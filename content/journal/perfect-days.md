Hirayama cleans Tokyo’s public toilets with the concentration of a craftsman. He wakes at the same hour, chooses a cassette for the drive, photographs light in the trees, and reads before sleep. Wim Wenders repeats these actions without treating them as proof that Hirayama has escaped ordinary pain. The routine gives his life shape; it does not make him invulnerable.

Kōji Yakusho’s performance is built from glances, posture, and the timing of a smile. Tokyo is observed at his scale: service roads, vending machines, parks, and brief encounters rather than postcard landmarks. Music and shifting light register the changes he will not put into words. The film finds dignity in his attention while leaving the cost of his solitude visible.
