Past Lives is not arranged as a contest between two men. Hae Sung carries Nora’s childhood and the language of the person she might have remained; Arthur shares the adult life she has actually made. Celine Song keeps all three characters morally present, especially Arthur, whose vulnerability never turns him into an obstacle or a villain.

The idea of in-yun gives Nora and Hae Sung a way to speak about connection, but the film does not use fate to solve anything for them. Distance is expressed physically—in doorways, across tables, and during the final wait beside the car. What hurts is not simply lost love. It is the recognition that migration creates versions of a life that cannot be reunited, even when the people can.
